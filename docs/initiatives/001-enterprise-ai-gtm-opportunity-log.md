# Initiative 001 — From Fast Delivery to Enterprise Value Realisation

> **Status:** Exploration / hypothesis discovery — not an approved PRD or solution architecture  
> **Date opened:** 2026-11-23  
> **Repository:** AI-GTM  
> **Version:** 0.1  
> **Purpose:** Preserve the reasoning, corrections, opportunities, constraints and falsifiable hypotheses emerging from the exploration of AI-driven enterprise operating models, post-delivery GTM, and value realisation.

---

## 0. Executive thesis

**Working question:** If Strategy, Continuous Discovery and AI-enabled Delivery make it dramatically easier to decide what to build and produce working capabilities, what constrains the enterprise from getting those capabilities into the hands of customers and capturing economic value?

This is **not** primarily a marketing-automation idea, a GEO monitoring tool, a chatbot business, or a proposal to replace an enterprise's technology estate. It is a search for the next bottleneck in **enterprise value creation** and for a possible repeatable business model to address it.

Our current *hypothesis* is that AI shifts constraints unevenly across the five classical management domains — **產、銷、人、發、財** (Production/Operations, Sales/Marketing, People/Organisation, R&D/Product, Finance). Reducing the cost or cycle time in one domain does not automatically improve the throughput of the whole business. Opportunities may emerge at the boundaries between domains: releasing and distributing capabilities, making proprietary expertise accessible, changing service economics, redesigning decision rights, and proving outcomes.

A potentially useful framing:

    Real customer / market signals
              ↓
    Strategic judgement and priorities
              ↓
    Continuous Discovery (the unknowns vary)
              ↓
    Execution / Delivery (increasingly AI-assisted)
              ↓
    Release / Market access / Distribution
              ↓
    Customer evaluation / Trust / Adoption / Payment
              ↓
    Business outcomes / Value evidence / Capital decisions
              ↺ Signals and strategy continuously update

This is **a map of relationships, not a mandatory stage-gate pipeline**. Signals may exist long before any initiative. A problem may already be well defined. Discovery, readiness and GTM activities can overlap. GTM is particularly important *after* delivery here because that is the question being explored, not because customer knowledge starts only after delivery.

**Provisional opportunity statement:** Make the path from *an enterprise capability that exists or can now be built rapidly* to *customer-recognised, trusted, adopted and economically valuable progress* more systematic, measurable and improvable.

## 1. Starting conditions and scope

### 1.1 What prompted the exploration

- The author has been developing a **Product Strategy OS plus continuous Discovery and Delivery Harness**. The assumed advantage is a tighter loop from strategic intent and evidence to reliable implementation.
- **Continuous customer interviews and discovery are already central**. This initiative should not invent a second, competing “market discovery lane” or insist that every idea needs the same discovery ceremony.
- AI-assisted implementation may make building software and assembling workflow capabilities cheaper and faster. This is a **directional premise to test in a specific context**, not proof that all enterprise delivery constraints have disappeared.
- The emerging role of **GTM Engineer** suggests that practices once associated with software engineering — data models, integrations, instrumentation, automated experiments, operational control — are moving into Sales/Marketing and Revenue Operations.
- The initial context is **one B2B professional-services or industrial enterprise with 200+ years of institutional history**, not an organisation managing 200 separate businesses. Its specialised expertise, relationships and legacy systems may be extensive and fragmented.
- The user is **not approaching this from deep marketing-domain expertise**. The distinctive angle is systems thinking, enterprise architecture, product strategy, discovery/delivery, cross-functional constraints and economic value.

### 1.2 What we are not assuming

- That marketing itself is the product, or that SEO/GEO dashboards are compelling standalone value.
- That a fully centralised Enterprise SSOT, knowledge graph, data lake or large-scale re-platforming must precede improvements.
- That every customer engagement starts with an idea, then problem definition, then sequential Discovery → Build → GTM.
- That “AI-native” means assigning an autonomous agent to each department.
- That tools, automation volume, content output, AI seats or model adoption are adequate measures of success.
- That faster engineering automatically means better revenue, profitability or customer outcomes.
- That enterprise-wide transformation is necessary before a meaningful small capability improvement.
- That every purchase is motivated by an explicit pain point alone; social, emotional and aspirational progress may also matter.

### 1.3 Scope boundaries

**In scope:** Product-to-market handoff; discoverability and distribution; GTM Engineering; expertise-to-customer information flow; customer trust and acquisition; adoption and expansion; cross-functional operating-model changes; value measurement; operating constraints; evidence-backed venture hypotheses.

**Out of scope for now:** Building a generic marketing suite; implementing a full ERP/CRM replacement; prescriptive technology stack; selecting an AI agent framework; claiming a finished startup model; executing automated outreach without consent and compliance design.

### 2.1 Terminology to keep distinct

- **Signal:** Observed evidence from customer behaviour, interviews, sales cycles, service requests, process workarounds, willingness to pay, regulation or market change. Not an idea generated in isolation.
- **Discovery:** Continuous reduction of *relevant* uncertainty. It may validate a known problem, a proposed solution, a new experience, a commercial assumption or an existing product's improvement.
- **Delivery:** Producing a working and reliable product/service/process capability.
- **Release:** Making that capability safely and operationally available — deployment, documentation, security, support readiness, controlled rollout.
- **Distribution:** Being encountered by the right buyers/users through credible channels, partners, search, existing accounts, referrals, procurement routes or sales.
- **GTM:** Commercially connecting an offering to an audience, context, proposition, proof, channel, buying motion and measurement. GTM is broader than advertising.
- **Activation/adoption:** The customer actually gets to and repeatedly experiences value — not merely seeing the offering.
- **Value realisation:** Defensible effect on customer progress and business outcomes such as retained revenue, margin, risk, time or expansion; not simply activity or savings claims.
- **Operational Excellence:** Improving the performance of an existing value stream.
- **Operating Model Transformation:** Changing responsibilities, cross-functional coordination, workflows, incentives and decision rights.
- **Business Model Transformation:** Changing how the business creates, delivers **and captures** economic value (e.g., pricing, productised services, channels).

## 3. Demand and buying psychology: the system begins outside the company

A product does not create demand merely by being technically impressive. The working mental model is **Jobs to Be Done / progress sought**: customers act when a proposed change is valuable enough relative to remaining with the status quo.

### 3.1 What can motivate a purchase?

1. **Functional:** Complete work faster; solve a constraint; reduce error, downtime or risk.
2. **Emotional:** Feel more confident, secure, in control or less uncertain.
3. **Social/organisational:** Demonstrate competence, satisfy peers, maintain standing or protect a team.
4. **Aspirational/new experience:** Achieve something that was previously impractical, not necessarily remove a painful defect.

The *Forces of Progress* offer one usable interview lens:

- **Push:** Frustration, change pressure, urgency or dissatisfaction with the current situation.
- **Pull:** Appeal of a better outcome or experience.
- **Habit:** Familiarity, existing contract, internal politics and current workflow.
- **Anxiety:** Risk of change, procurement, data exposure, trust, implementation or career consequences.

For B2B, distinguish **user, champion, technical evaluator, economic buyer, procurement, legal/security and executive sponsor**. These people may experience different jobs, risks and incentives. A user who likes a product is not necessarily the person who can approve or fund it.

### 3.2 Signals that may exist before product readiness

- Repeated, independently observed customer requests or support escalations.
- Persistent spreadsheet/manual workarounds and expensive handoffs.
- Sales conversations failing at the same evidence, risk or technical-access question.
- Customers actively seeking substitutes, switching suppliers or paying for manual work.
- Increases in relevant search, partner, procurement or tender activity.
- Regulatory, technology or buyer-behaviour changes that alter the cost of remaining unchanged.
- Desired experiences users attempt to assemble from disconnected tools.
- Repeated account-expansion requests in existing B2B relationships.

**Caution:** Complaint frequency, search volume and interview enthusiasm are weak proxies for willingness to pay. Seek evidence of *committed resources, changed behaviour or purchasing action*.

### 3.3 What Discovery should be allowed to do

Discovery might begin with a **known problem**, a **known buyer need**, an **unexplored new experience**, an **existing service to improve**, or a **technical possibility**. It should identify the next uncertainty worth reducing rather than force every case through an identical problem-definition phase.

Important separation: continuous discovery can reduce the risk of building the wrong capability; it **does not by itself guarantee distribution access, procurement approval, buyer trust or monetisation**. Those are different uncertainties and constraints. This is a boundary, not an argument for a separate mandatory discovery lane.

## 4. Enterprise perspective: 產、銷、人、發、財

The five domains are **different lenses on one interconnected value stream**, not five independent AI departments to automate.

| Domain | AI can make cheaper/faster | Constraint that may move or become more visible | Cross-domain opportunity | Watch-out |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **產 — Production / Operations / Service Delivery** | Drafting, software, document production, analysis, routine execution, QA assistance | Reliability, integration, regulated approvals, delivery accountability, real-world throughput | Productise repeatable services; enable customer-facing teams with operational expertise | More output without demand; quality and liability; billable-hour cannibalisation |
| **銷 — Sales / Marketing / Commercial** | Account research, segmentation, content adaptation, lead routing, CRM workflows | Attention, trust, distribution rights, procurement, timing, multiple stakeholders | GTM Engineering; expertise-to-commercial-activation | High-volume low-trust spam; vanity leads; misattribution |
| **人 — People / Organisation** | Administrative and routine knowledge work, training, information access | Adoption, role redesign, incentives, ownership, decision rights | Human-agent operating practices; cross-functional outcome teams | Hidden shadow work, deskilling, accountability gaps |
| **發 — R&D / Product / Innovation** | Research synthesis, prototypes, testing, experiment execution | Strategic selection, domain judgment, scientific validity, scarce customer access | Stronger link between signal, Discovery, product capability and realised benefit | Experiment throughput mistaken for learning |
| **財 — Finance / Capital Allocation** | Reporting, scenario analysis, forecasting assistance, anomaly identification | Causal ROI measurement, pricing model, budget flexibility, risk appetite | Continuous value evaluation and evidence-informed resource allocation | “Hours saved” booked as cash; optimism bias; double-counted benefits |

### 4.1 The cross-functional effect of one improvement

**Illustrative scenario, not an observed result:** AI reduces specialist analysis time from 100 hours to 20.

- **Production:** Capacity increases, but who assures correctness?
- **Sales:** Do we offer faster turnaround, lower price, better service or a new package?
- **People:** Are specialists freed for higher-value tasks, or is the new workflow undermining training?
- **R&D/Product:** Can repeated analysis become a standardised or self-service product?
- **Finance:** Are 80 hours *real savings*, redeployable capacity, or merely less billable revenue?

Optimising only the Production metric could reduce revenue in a time-and-materials model. **Local efficiency is not necessarily enterprise value.**

### 4.2 Operational Excellence versus transformation

- **Operational Excellence:** Improve a single workflow with clear owner, customer impact and controls.
- **Operating Model Transformation:** Coordinate changes to the workflow **and** its ownership, incentives, staffing, service-level expectations and decisions.
- **Business Model Transformation:** Reconfigure product/service packaging, price, distribution or revenue economics.

These are not maturity levels that must occur in order. A small improvement can become evidence for a deeper change. Many improvements should remain small.

## 5. Post-delivery: the specific GTM problem under investigation

### 5.1 Release ≠ distribution ≠ adoption ≠ revenue

| Boundary | Question | Evidence worth collecting |
|---|---|---|
| Release | Can an approved target customer actually access and use the offering? | Deployment availability, support readiness, successful onboarding, approval lead time |
| Discoverability / Distribution | Can the right customer discover it **in their normal buying context**? | Qualified reach, relevant referrals, target-account engagement, source quality |
| Evaluation / Trust | Do they understand applicability and believe the claim? | Evidence consumption, technical queries answered, stakeholder objections resolved |
| Conversion | Can interest progress through the buying process? | Qualified opportunities, stage progression, sales-cycle friction, cost of acquisition |
| Adoption | Does the customer achieve the desired progress? | Time-to-first-value, sustained meaningful usage, realised customer benefit |
| Value | Does the result justify customer and provider economics? | Margin, retention, expansion, attributable incremental revenue, risk/cost adjusted outcomes |

A release announcement does not create distribution. Distribution does not ensure a buying decision. A conversion does not guarantee adoption or retention.

### 5.2 B2B professional-services journey — logical responsibilities, not fixed steps

**External Discovery** (search, AI assistants, tenders, partners, trusted experts) → **Entry/Context** → **Problem/Capability Navigation** → **Relevance Matching** → **Evidence & Trust** → **Commercial CTA/Handoff** → **Sales/Procurement/Delivery** → **Relationship, Adoption & Learning**.

A customer may enter anywhere; an existing account may skip discovery entirely. The user asked to keep these stages **conceptually separate**, especially external discovery, on-site discovery, service matching and CTA.

Examples of legitimate CTAs in complex B2B: request a technical assessment, ask for a specialist, start an RFP, explore a scoped pilot, seek a feasibility review or arrange an NDA. “Book demo” is not a universal answer.

### 5.3 Why GTM Engineering is relevant

The emergent **GTM Engineer** builds technical commercial systems: CRM and enrichment integrations, account/intent data flows, workflow automation, routing, experimentation, observability and governance. It is not simply an AI copywriting role.

Potential relation to the Strategy/Discovery/Delivery Harness:

- **Shared primitives:** Explicit hypotheses, artefact versions, tool use, evaluation, guardrails, orchestration, evidence and feedback.
- **Different correctness:** Software tests can inspect implementation correctness; GTM results depend on market behaviour and counterfactuals. Engagement, conversion and revenue cannot be unit-tested into existence.
- **Different permissions:** CRM writes, customer contact, external publishing, consent, brand claims, segmentation, spend and data use require narrower approval gates than read-only research.
- **Different outputs:** Deliverable capability versus market activation, buyer progress, reliable signals and commercial outcomes.

This suggests a **possibly reusable execution/evidence infrastructure**, not proof that one generic “GTM agent loop” should run everything.

## 6. Legacy technology, information readiness and enterprise constraints

A mature B2B enterprise may hold valuable expertise across PLM, ERP, CRM, CMS, knowledge repositories, engineering files, contracts, sales conversations and individual experts. Systems are often messy. **Readiness is a real constraint, but global re-platforming is not the default solution.**

### 6.1 Information-to-market gap

An enterprise can **possess** technical capability without making it **findable, understandable, legally publishable or commercially actionable**.

Examples:

- Internal service names differ from the customer's problem language.
- Engineering proof lives behind systems the sales team cannot query.
- Case studies omit context, permitted claims or outcome evidence.
- Product/service metadata is too generic to support intent-based navigation.
- Sales handoff drops problem context and requires the customer to repeat it.

This is a **knowledge-to-market** challenge as much as an AI/search challenge.

### 6.2 Better implementation unit: a bounded vertical slice

Choose **one customer segment + one signal/need + one relevant capability + one existing system-of-record path + one customer-facing touchpoint + one economic outcome**.

For that slice:
1. Identify the authoritative source(s), owners, freshness and permitted-use scope.
2. Establish minimal contracts/interfaces and a traceable evidence trail.
3. Convert expert knowledge into customer-intelligible, approved information.
4. Connect to the specific discovery, service-matching, sales or onboarding workflow.
5. Measure what changed, including human workload and quality.
6. Reuse proven integration patterns only when a second slice needs them.

Do **not** infer that a single canonical SSOT is required. Sometimes federation and local source ownership are more practical. Reusable shared semantics may emerge gradually.

### 6.3 Hard constraints that AI does not erase

- Safety, regulated professional advice, technical certification and liability.
- Security, confidentiality, data residency, information permissions and lawful customer contact.
- Legacy API availability, poor identifiers and inconsistent product/service taxonomy.
- Human approvals, procurement contracts, client-specific requirements and channel access.
- Incentive conflicts between revenue, billable utilisation, efficiency and customer success.
- Brand trust, evidence quality and reputational downside from incorrect claims.
- Finite customer attention and willingness to change.
- Ability to demonstrate incremental benefit versus a credible baseline.

## 7. Constraint theory and blind spots

**Working heuristic:** locate the constraint governing *end-to-end throughput*, not the loudest or most expensive function.

| Assumption to challenge | Why it could fail | Test/response |
|---|---|---|
| “Build is no longer a bottleneck.” | AI lowers code-writing cost but not review, domain QA, deployment, certification or adoption. | Measure complete idea-to-customer-value lead time; separate coding time from cycle time. |
| “More features create more growth.” | Customers have limited attention and switching capacity. | Compare feature output with qualified usage, retention and revenue. |
| “Customer interviews mean GTM risk is solved.” | Interviewees may not represent buyers or channels; willingness to pay differs from expressed preference. | Trace buyer roles, procurement, committed action and actual purchase paths. |
| “If data is connected, knowledge is ready.” | Information may be wrong, stale, private or not phrased for customer problems. | Audit provenance, permissions, evidence and problem-language mapping. |
| “AI adoption proves transformation.” | Tool usage can coexist with unchanged workflows and weak economics. | Track handoffs, decision latency, customer outcomes and real net benefit. |
| “Time saved equals money saved.” | Freed hours may be unredeployed, billing may drop, and AI systems add costs. | Distinguish cash avoided, capacity released, revenue impact and reinvestment. |
| “GTM Engineering means more automated outreach.” | Volume can reduce trust and breach data/marketing rules. | Prioritise signal quality, consent, buyer utility and outcome per contact. |
| “Enterprise AI platform first.” | Big-bang data/architecture work can consume capital before evidence. | Require a bounded slice with measurable customer and economic value. |
| “One department owns the value.” | Gains and losses appear in different departments; decisions are politically distributed. | Assign one accountable business outcome owner and explicit decision rights. |
| “A universal agent can optimise all functions.” | Different risk models and evaluability; impossible to verify many autonomous claims. | Bounded workflows, risk-based human approvals, audit and escalation. |
| “AI moat = model or automation.” | Models and tooling commoditise; enterprise access and trust are costly to win. | Test whether unique data rights, integration, workflow position, outcomes or distribution persist. |

### Additional disconfirming perspectives

- **No-new-software hypothesis:** Existing CRM/ERP/product analytics and a process change may solve the problem at lower total cost than a new platform.
- **Consulting trap:** Every customer needs bespoke taxonomy, integrations, approvals and GTM strategy; little reusable IP accrues.
- **Incentive trap:** Sponsors want lower costs while functional leaders are rewarded for utilisation or existing revenue streams.
- **Attribution trap:** Long-cycle B2B wins cannot be causally linked to an AI-generated interaction with confidence.
- **Productisation trap:** A high-touch service depends on tacit judgment, customer context or professional liability and cannot be safely productised.
- **Distribution trap:** A proprietary enterprise platform might have weak market reach even if its internal execution engine is excellent.

### Research interpretation

The first two levels — **AI-enabled operational improvement** and **cross-functional operating-model change** — have tangible examples. A widely adopted, independent **Enterprise Value Creation OS product category** is **still a hypothesis**, not an established market fact.

The cases must not be treated as interchangeable. A global bank, consumer-goods company, advertising group and industrial vendor have different economics, governance and buyer behaviour.

| Candidate | Customer pain / promised outcome | Initial buyer | Delivery model | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Expertise-to-Market Activation** | Make existing B2B specialist capabilities findable, understandable, provable and actionable for specific buyer problems. | Commercial/Product/Service leadership | Bounded integration + content/knowledge workflow + measurement | Looks like bespoke content consultancy; weak differentiation |

| **D. AI Operations / Transformation Engineering** | Identify and improve one value-stream constraint, including cross-functional workflow and ownership. | COO / business-unit owner | Service-led operational improvement; repeatable playbooks | Labour intensity, customer-specific work |

**Provisional near-term research focus (not a product decision):** **A + B + C** at the intersection of expert knowledge, post-delivery GTM, and measurable outcomes. **D** may be the services channel to learn quickly. **E** is an architectural north star, not an MVP.

### Decision criteria for selecting a wedge

- Intensity of a repeated, economically significant pain.
- Identifiable owner with authority and budget.
- Observable customer-facing outcome in a realistic test window.
- Access to existing systems, experts and safe data.
- Repeatability across clients without extensive custom consulting.
- Ability to validate demand with buyer commitment, not only enthusiasm.
- Defensibility beyond prompt templates and generic automations.
- Manageable legal, security and brand exposure.

## 10. Hypotheses — explicit, falsifiable, not yet proven

All numeric thresholds below are **illustrative experiment gates**. Adjust only after measuring the baseline and customer-specific sales cycle. Record failures with the same discipline as successes.

| ID | Falsifiable hypothesis | Low-cost experiment | Leading / outcome evidence | What would weaken or reject it? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **H01 — Post-build bottleneck** | For a defined B2B value stream with fast AI-assisted Delivery, customer access, distribution, trust or buying handoff consumes more elapsed time than implementation. | Trace 5–10 recent offering/feature releases end to end; time each handoff. | Median elapsed time and blocked days by segment; time-to-first-customer-value. | Engineering/reliability remains the dominant constraint or post-delivery delay is immaterial. |
| **H02 — Existing unmet market signals** | Useful commercially actionable signals already exist in sales/service/operations records before formal product readiness. | With permission, sample 20–30 recent customer conversations/tickets/failed proposals; trace signals to subsequent action. | Repeated independent needs, named buyers, urgency, workarounds, evidence of spend. | Signals are anecdotal, inaccessible, unrepresentative or lack buyer action. |
| **H03 — Expertise-to-market gap** | One approved slice of specialist knowledge mapped to customer problem-language improves relevant customer understanding and next-step completion. | Create one problem-led offering/evidence experience using existing content; compare against current page/workflow. | Correctness review, qualified inquiries, technical question resolution, task completion. | No comprehension/handoff improvement, or content governance cost overwhelms benefit. |
| **H04 — Release-to-GTM handoff** | A reusable release-to-market workflow reduces internal handoff time without increasing misleading claims or outreach risk. | Instrument one launch; automate only evidence-approved, reversible tasks with human publication approval. | Time from release to approved channel activation; rework; error/complaint rate. | Manual tailoring remains necessary; cycle time unchanged; quality declines. |
| **H05 — GTM Engineer as a buyer need** | Commercial teams will fund engineering of data, routing and workflow reliability, not just buy copy/content generation. | Interview 10–15 GTM/RevOps leaders; observe current workflows; request a paid scoped pilot. | Budget owner, quantified workflow loss, willingness to pay, paid pilot. | Positive interest without budget/action, or existing CRM tools solve cheaply. |
| **H06 — Value evidence differentiator** | Linking release, buyer progress and economics improves priority/kill decisions more than isolated activity dashboards. | For 2–3 strategic bets, create a manual evidence ledger and review it with a decision owner. | Decisions changed, spend reallocated, avoided work, faster resolution of uncertainty. | No consequential decisions change; attribution too weak to be useful. |
| **H07 — Services to repeatable product** | At least two organisations share a core repeatable workflow/data contract that can be served without rebuilding each integration from scratch. | Repeat the same bounded slice in two distinct B2B contexts; log common versus client-specific hours. | Share of reusable components, deployment effort, implementation gross margin. | Customer-specific semantics/approval dominate; every engagement is bespoke. |
| **H08 — Cross-domain incentive constraint** | For selected AI improvements, decision rights or pricing/incentives constrain benefit more than missing model capability. | Map one end-to-end approval/value flow with 產銷人發財 owners; inspect one disputed investment or launch. | Decision latency, unclear ownership, conflicting KPIs, time to agreement. | Constraints are primarily technical, not organisational. |
| **H09 — B2B trust over scale** | Verified domain evidence and high-context access outperform higher volumes of generic AI-generated outreach for qualified buyer progress. | Compare small, consent-compliant, account-relevant approaches with current baseline; avoid uncontrolled spamming. | Meaningful replies, qualified technical meetings, stakeholder progress, opt-outs. | Evidence-rich approach shows no difference and costs more per qualified opportunity. |
| **H10 — Economic value capture** | A material share of AI time savings is not realised as enterprise benefit without pricing, redeployment or process change. | Follow one efficiency improvement through billing, staffing, risk, delivery quality and margin. | Cash cost avoided, capacity redeployed, incremental margin or revenue. | Savings are already fully captured and correctly recognised. |

### How to evaluate evidence

- **Observation:** A real behaviour, event, workflow trace or transaction.
- **Interpretation:** Our causal story about the observation.
- **Decision:** Continue, change scope, stop or investigate.
- **Confidence:** Low / medium / high, with a stated reason.
- **Counterevidence:** Record what did *not* happen and competing explanations.

Do not “confirm” a hypothesis because an LLM generates a plausible market narrative.

## 11. First experiments — intentionally non-platform

**Experiment 0 — Map a real value stream (1 candidate company / offering).**

Find a single recently delivered service feature or new offering. Reconstruct:

    Earliest buyer/customer signal
           → commercial and product decisions
           → discovery evidence
           → implementation / release
           → website/partner/account accessibility
           → sales handoff and trust objections
           → first use / adoption
           → customer benefit and provider economics

Measure handoff delay and assign one accountable owner to each decision. The aim is to **locate the actual constraint**, not pitch an AI tool.

**Experiment 1 — One customer problem, one expertise-to-market slice.**

Select a problem for which expertise exists but customer-facing access is weak. Build a minimally sufficient approved evidence set, a problem-led navigation path and a commercially appropriate CTA. Compare task completion/qualified handoff with baseline. Do not build a global knowledge graph.

**Experiment 2 — One release-to-GTM workflow.**

Use existing CMS/CRM and human-controlled approval to turn an approved release into a targeted customer-facing activation: approved claims, target segment, channel packaging, routing and telemetry. Inspect quality and speed. Explicitly exclude unsolicited broad outreach.

**Experiment 3 — Manual value evidence ledger.**

Track a small number of strategic bets from initial signal through buyer/action evidence and operational cost. Ask whether the ledger changes any decision. **Do not build a dashboard until there is a user and decision it serves.**

**Experiment 4 — Commercial commitment.**

Interview budget owners after showing an actual bounded result. Request a narrow paid engagement or explicit resource commitment. Treat non-commitment as evidence against immediate monetisation even if the idea is praised.

## 12. Measures that prevent false progress

**Delivery and release:** Lead time to production-ready capability, escaped defects, rework, approval wait, support burden.

**Distribution and GTM:** Qualified audience reached, relevance of source, time from offering approval to market access, lead-routing accuracy, account-stage progression, complaints/opt-outs.

**Adoption and customer progress:** Time-to-first-value, repeated meaningful usage, completed real-world jobs, stakeholder trust and objections, retention/expansion.

**Enterprise value:** Incremental contribution margin, customer lifetime impact with uncertainty, net cost to serve, billable-hour effect, real redeployment, cash costs and risk impact.

**Cross-functional operations:** Decision latency, handoff count, end-to-end throughput, bottleneck location, owner clarity and change approval time.

**Avoid treating as outcomes:** Number of agents, prompts, content pieces, features shipped, AI licences, automation runs or dashboard views.

**Attribution discipline:** Separate (a) direct instrumentation, (b) plausible directional association, and (c) causal estimates with a credible comparison. For long-cycle B2B, use cohort and process evidence where controlled experiments are not feasible; do not fabricate certainty.

## 13. Operating and commercial models worth comparing

### Option 1 — Expert advisory / transformation consultancy
- **Revenue:** Discovery/audit and implementation fees.
- **Benefit:** Fast access to real constraints; can test without a large platform.
- **Failure mode:** Non-repeatable bespoke work and low leverage.

### Option 2 — Productised transformation / managed GTM Engineering
- **Revenue:** Scoped implementation plus recurring managed workflow and outcome review.
- **Benefit:** Learns repeatable integration and decision patterns.
- **Failure mode:** Hidden manual operations; unclear outcome attribution or scope creep.

### Option 3 — Enterprise software / evidence orchestration
- **Revenue:** Subscription or usage, potentially services for integration.
- **Benefit:** Reusable infrastructure around specific workflows.
- **Failure mode:** Generic platform with no clear buyer; competing with CRM, BI, ERP and workflow products; costly enterprise security review.

### Option 4 — Outcome-linked commercial arrangement
- **Revenue:** Fees linked to an agreed observable result.
- **Benefit:** Strong alignment if metrics are reliable.
- **Failure mode:** Delayed or contested attribution; external factors; adverse incentives; customer confidentiality.

**Working recommendation for research:** Validate a **repeatable narrow service/workflow** and its budget owner before claiming to build an Enterprise AI Transformation OS. Preserve the possibility that the best result is an operating methodology rather than standalone software.

## 14. Architecture implications — principles only, not a technical commitment

1. **Outcome before automation:** Specify customer progress, business owner, quality bar and expected value.
2. **Signals before “ideas”:** Allow evidence to enter the system from any trusted source at any time.
3. **Variable discovery:** Validate the uncertainty that actually exists. Do not demand a fixed two-lane process.
4. **Systems of record remain authoritative:** CRM, CMS, PLM, ERP and other enterprise systems keep source ownership.
5. **Federated, slice-first integrations:** Define minimal contracts, provenance, permissions, freshness and reversibility.
6. **Human decision rights:** Publishing, customer contact, pricing, spend, legal claims and consequential writes require appropriate authorisation.
7. **Separate readiness states:** Technically released, discoverable, commercially executable and economically successful are not interchangeable.
8. **Composable capability:** Existing Product Strategy OS / Discovery / Delivery may supply shared logging, evaluation and orchestration primitives, not a mandate to force all GTM into a coding-agent runtime.
9. **Measure entire value flow:** Optimise the end-to-end constraint, not the number of tasks completed by a single team.
10. **Small before large:** Demonstrate value in one vertical slice before investing in general frameworks or enterprise platform.

## 15. Open questions and research backlog

### Buyer and market
- Who experiences this pain most acutely: a long-established professional-services firm, an industrial supplier, a technical consultancy or a software-enabled services business?
- Which role actually budgets for cross-functional value realisation: CEO, COO, CPO, CRO, business-unit leader or transformation office?
- Is the motivating pain a **failed commercial handoff**, **unfindable expertise**, **slow release-to-market**, **poor adoption**, or **unprovable financial benefit**?
- Would a buyer pay for a diagnostic, a managed workflow, a repeatable product, or no new purchase?

### Workflow and technical reality
- What systems can be accessed for a legally compliant pilot?
- Are the necessary claims, professional evidence and case studies publishable?
- Do product usage and CRM systems provide a shared account and offering identifier?
- What is the minimum reliable integration; what is the security/review burden?
- Can the next bottleneck be improved **without** a new AI model at all?

### Economics and organisation
- How do pricing and billable-hours incentives change if delivery effort falls?
- Who benefits from saved time, and who bears the integration/support cost?
- Who owns cross-functional approval to move from operational improvement to a new offering?
- Are buyers willing to make a paid commitment within a realistic procurement cycle?
- Is the data advantage or workflow position defendable against existing enterprise vendors?

### Case-study verification (sources and caveats)
- Examine DBS's economic-value methodology, not just the headline figure.
- Compare WPP Open investments/adoption with subsequent reported commercial outcomes.
- Separate Siemens–Salesforce announced capabilities and demos from measured customer results.
- Review industry-specific barriers before generalising from banking to professional services.
- Validate GTM Engineer job-market evidence independently of tools that promote that label.
- Look for **failed** transformations, particularly cases of increased tooling spend without changes in throughput or profit.

## 18. Next research decision

**Do not ask “Which AI GTM product should we code?” yet.** Ask:

> **In a real B2B enterprise where the ability to build a capability is improving, what specific constraint now prevents a known customer need from becoming a trusted, adopted and profitable outcome — and will someone pay to remove that constraint?**

The best first initiative is a **single end-to-end value-stream trace plus one bounded improvement experiment**, followed by a **commercial commitment test**. If the same bottleneck repeats across enterprises and the workflow is reusable, then consider a product architecture. If it is organisation-specific, the correct outcome may be a consulting method, internal capability, or a reason **not** to build a startup.
